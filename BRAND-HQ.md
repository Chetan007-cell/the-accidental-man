# The Accidental Man — Brand HQ

**Status:** Source of truth for brand and content work  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Use:** Keep this document in the project and consult it before planning, writing, repurposing, or reviewing content.

> **A man isn't born. He's built.**

> **A factory for the modern man — to become, maintain, and evolve.**

## 1. Brand foundation

### Positioning

The Accidental Man is a practical, thoughtful content brand that helps men build and care for a fuller life. It is a factory for the modern man: a place to learn, reflect, practice, maintain, and evolve across the parts of life that make a person whole.

### Philosophy

Manhood is not a fixed status, performance, or checklist. It is built through choices, habits, responsibility, self-knowledge, relationships, and the willingness to keep learning. Men do not need to be perfect to begin; they need useful tools and room to grow.

### Mission

Help men become more capable, grounded, healthy, present, and intentional through clear ideas and practical guidance for everyday life.

### Vision

A world where men can grow without shame, care for themselves and others, and define a life they respect.

### Brand promise

Every piece should leave the reader with at least one of these: a clearer perspective, a useful next step, a question worth considering, or the feeling that growth is possible.

### What the brand is / is not

- **Is:** thoughtful, useful, human, curious, and grounded in real life.
- **Is:** for men at different ages, backgrounds, and stages—not one narrow ideal.
- **Is not:** an alpha-male, dominance, hustle-at-all-costs, or guru brand.
- **Is not:** a claim to have life completely figured out. Be honest about what is experience, opinion, or evidence.

## 2. Audience

Men who want to become more intentional and capable but may not have had good models, practical guidance, or a welcoming place to ask basic questions. They may be building confidence, health, emotional maturity, personal style, work, money habits, relationships, or a sense of direction. They value directness without humiliation and ambition without bravado.

Write to one thoughtful person, not to “men” as a stereotype. Avoid assuming age, income, relationship status, sexuality, culture, body type, or life path.

## 3. Content universe and pillars

Use these nine areas as the content universe. A piece can touch more than one, but give it one primary pillar.

1. **Mind:** learning, attention, habits, judgment, confidence, and mental clarity.
2. **Emotions:** emotional literacy, resilience, self-respect, asking for help, and coping skills.
3. **Body:** movement, strength, sleep, nutrition, health, and sustainable care.
4. **Spirit and purpose:** values, meaning, identity, reflection, and the questions that guide a life. Treat belief traditions respectfully; do not prescribe one.
5. **Grooming:** simple, inclusive routines and practical care.
6. **Style:** fit, function, self-expression, and dressing for real situations and budgets.
7. **Career and money:** work, craft, financial basics, decisions, and long-term stability. Be precise about limits and evidence.
8. **Relationships:** friendship, family, partnership, communication, boundaries, care, and accountability.
9. **Life:** home, time, responsibility, transitions, culture, leisure, and putting the pieces together.

## 4. Voice and tone

**Voice:** a thoughtful older brother with the discipline of an editor: candid, warm, composed, practical, and curious.

- Use plain language, short paragraphs, specific examples, and concrete actions.
- Sound confident where the ground is solid; show uncertainty where it is not.
- Invite reflection without preaching. Encourage accountability without contempt.
- Use humor lightly and never at a vulnerable person's expense.
- Speak to the reader as an adult. No fake urgency or manufactured intimacy.
- Prefer “try,” “consider,” and “here is what helped” when advice depends on context.

### Avoid

“Real men do X”; “high-value male”; “alpha/beta”; shame as motivation; gender-war bait; miracle transformations; empty grind culture; exaggerated certainty; and calling ordinary setbacks weakness. Do not imply that buying a product makes someone a better man.

## 5. Editorial rules

1. Start with a real question, tension, observation, or useful promise.
2. Make one main point. Support it with reasoning, an example, a story, or trustworthy evidence.
3. Give the reader a proportionate next step. Avoid pretending one habit solves a complex problem.
4. Separate fact, interpretation, and personal experience. Verify claims, especially health, money, safety, and legal claims; cite credible sources when used.
5. Do not invent personal stories, quotations, credentials, studies, statistics, or outcomes. Mark any first-person detail that needs the author's confirmation.
6. Respect privacy and dignity. Remove identifying details from stories unless there is explicit permission.
7. Keep advice inclusive of different bodies, budgets, cultures, abilities, and circumstances.
8. Edit for usefulness, clarity, originality, and a natural human voice. AI drafts are starting material, not automatically publishable copy.
9. Use a clear title and a specific call to action only when it serves the reader.
10. For sensitive or high-stakes topics, stay within the writer's competence and point readers toward qualified help where appropriate.

## 6. Visual and content principles

- Aim for **considered, modern, editorial, human**—not macho caricature or luxury posturing.
- Favor natural, lived-in settings; varied and believable men; calm composition; legible typography; and a restrained, consistent palette.
- Show practice, process, and everyday life, not only polished “after” states.
- Avoid stock clichés (angry man, lone wolf, supercar, staged dominance) and visuals that equate worth with physique or wealth.
- Make every social asset understandable on its own, readable on a phone, and consistent with the core idea.
- Establish exact colors, typefaces, logo use, and image treatments from the existing visual identity; do not invent brand specifications when they are not documented.

## 7. Operating model

Use one Brand HQ and a small set of repeatable workflows. Keep one core idea at the center, then adapt it for each channel. One chat per platform can help organize ongoing work, but do not create a new agent or chat for every post. Preserve this HQ as the shared reference.

**Idea → strategy brief → researched/approved source → flagship article or script → platform adaptations → publish → learn.**

Before drafting, supply the workflow with the relevant section of this HQ, the audience, the desired outcome, and any source material. Ask it to flag missing facts and author-only details instead of inventing them.

## 8. Reusable workflows and prompts

Replace bracketed fields. Attach or paste the approved source material. For every workflow, tell the AI to follow this HQ, avoid unsupported claims, identify assumptions, and ask for missing facts only when they block a useful draft.

### A. Content Strategy

**Use for:** choosing a useful idea, angle, audience need, and publishing priority.

```text
Act as the content strategist for The Accidental Man. Follow Brand HQ.
Audience/context: [who and what stage]
Goal: [awareness, usefulness, conversation, subscriber growth, etc.]
Recent content / topics to avoid repeating: [list]
Available time and channels: [constraints]

Suggest 5 distinct content ideas. For each, give: audience problem or question, primary pillar, specific angle, why it fits our brand, evidence or first-hand input needed, best format, and a practical reader takeaway. Then recommend one idea and explain the choice briefly. Favor usefulness and originality over volume or clickbait. Do not make up audience data.
```

### B. Editorial Writing

**Use for:** a flagship article, essay, or practical guide.

```text
Act as an editor and writer for The Accidental Man. Follow Brand HQ.
Brief: [approved idea, reader, purpose, key point]
Sources / notes: [paste links, notes, or interview material]
Author's real experience to include (if any): [details or “none supplied”]
Target length and format: [specification]

First return a compact outline and list any claims that need verification or first-person facts only the author can provide. Do not invent either. Then draft with a clear opening, one central argument, useful examples, practical steps, and a grounded ending. Label citations or verification needs. Keep the voice human, direct, nuanced, and free of guru claims. After the draft, include a short self-edit checklist of accuracy, clarity, inclusivity, and originality.
```

### C. Social Repurposing

**Use for:** adapting an approved article or idea for Instagram and LinkedIn.

```text
Repurpose this approved source for The Accidental Man, following Brand HQ.
Source: [paste article or approved core idea]
Primary reader and goal: [details]
Channels and count: [e.g. one 7-slide Instagram carousel, one caption, one LinkedIn post]

Keep the central meaning accurate, but make each version native to its channel rather than copying the same wording. For each, provide the opening hook, complete draft, visual or layout direction, and a restrained CTA. Do not add facts or claims absent from the source. Make each asset useful on its own. Flag any excerpt that needs context.
```

### D. YouTube

**Use for:** a video essay, practical explainer, or short video derived from an approved idea.

```text
Create a YouTube concept for The Accidental Man using Brand HQ.
Approved idea/source: [paste]
Audience question: [question]
Format and target length: [long-form / short, minutes]
Available footage, host experience, and sources: [details]

Return: 3 accurate title options (no deceptive clickbait), thumbnail concept, opening 15 seconds, structured script with spoken language and visual/B-roll cues, one practical takeaway, close/CTA, and a short source/verification list. Do not invent host anecdotes, credentials, footage, or results. Flag any section that needs the host's own experience. Keep the tone grounded and engaging.
```

### E. Newsletter

**Use for:** a brief note that deepens one idea and earns a reader's attention.

```text
Draft a newsletter for The Accidental Man, following Brand HQ.
Core idea or approved source: [paste]
Reader context: [what they may be facing]
Purpose and desired length: [details]
Links or resources: [approved items]

Give a specific subject line and preview text, then a warm, concise email with one central idea, a useful reflection or action, and one relevant link or reply invitation. Keep it personal without inventing personal experience. No fake urgency, exaggerated promises, or unrelated promotion. Flag anything the author must personalize or verify.
```

### F. Analytics and learning

**Use for:** monthly review, with small samples treated cautiously.

```text
Review this content-period data for The Accidental Man using Brand HQ.
Period and baseline: [dates/context]
Posts and metrics: [paste; include platform, format, reach/impressions, clicks, saves, shares, comments, watch time/completion, email signups where available]
Effort or distribution changes: [what was done]

Summarize what happened without treating small samples as proof. Separate observation from interpretation. Identify the strongest signals, weak or missing data, possible explanations, and 2–3 low-cost experiments for the next period. Compare like with like when possible. Do not infer audience demographics or causes from metrics that do not support them. End with what to keep, change, and stop testing.
```

## 9. Review checklist before publishing

- Does it clearly serve a real reader and fit one primary pillar?
- Is the main point understandable in one sentence?
- Are factual claims supported and current enough for the topic?
- Are personal claims genuinely the author's, and private details safe to share?
- Does the piece avoid shame, stereotypes, guru posturing, and inflated promises?
- Is the format native to its channel and accessible on a phone?
- Does it give the reader a worthwhile takeaway?
- Is the call to action relevant and proportionate?
